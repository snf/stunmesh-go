Filtered YAML configuration fuzz diagnostic (2026-09-13 UTC)

The original coverage-guided config campaign found G-13, a reproducible
nil-mapping-key panic. A follow-up audit-only harness first YAML-decodes each
candidate and skips that known class before calling the unmodified loader.

The first follow-up was scheduled for one hour but was interrupted after
130 seconds by an investigator-sent SIGQUIT to a stalled fuzz worker to
capture its goroutine stack. The fuzzer reported EOF while minimizing and
saved corpus hash 0512e6f6c90645a6. That input was rerun and passed
immediately; the failure was induced by our diagnostic signal, not a
reproduced source defect. It must not be counted as a discovered crash.

A scratch-only diagnostic rerun with a goroutine-stack timer and the same
filtered target completed three minutes successfully: 925,819 executions,
418 interesting cases, PASS. Captured worker stacks during zero-execution
intervals were in internal/fuzz.minimizeBytes and the audit-only YAML
prefilter's yaml.Unmarshal. No per-input two-second watchdog artifact or
independent panic was produced. This bounds the finding: Go fuzz minimization
and repeated parsing made the campaign slow, but the diagnostic did not
establish a production YAML parser hang. The complete transient logs and
stack captures remain in the separate isolated audit workspace, not this
repository. Further time-bounded filtered fuzzing is planned.
